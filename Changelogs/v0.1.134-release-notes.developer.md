## 2. The two defects

**Read/write conflation.** `adjacency is not authorization` did not distinguish
inspection from mutation. It collided with `read before write` and `verify code`
in `# method`, and with `diagnose first error` in `# build discipline`, all of
which oblige the agent to inspect things the user did not name. The obedient agent
could not check, and `verify code` forbids assuming, so a checkable fact was
downgraded to a hedge purely by rule interaction.

The precedence chain does not resolve it. `honesty > scope > blast radius >
brevity` arbitrates between conflicting rules, not the permitted radius of a read.

**Quarantine deadlock.** `prefer quarantine to deletion` in `# method` mandates a
labelled holding path. `no unrequested actions` in `# scope` forbids extra files
nobody asked for, and `# scope` outranks `# method`. No carve-out existed.
Predicted behaviour is a stall.

## 3. Method, and what each finding is worth

44 agents across three concurrent phases, with adversarial verification.

| finding | method | strength |
|---|---|---|
| tally is 20/18/20/16, not 15 vs 6 | 13 agents classifying every rule | **strong**, replaces a hand-count |
| `adjacency` is the dominant harm | cause in 4 of 5 harmful scenarios; 32 conflicts, 7 high | **strong**, three independent methods converged |
| `quarantine` deadlocks | classify and interact, independently | **strong**, a plain logical contradiction |
| the other three rules | interact flagged them, scenarios did not | **weak**, unverified |
| 10 of 10 findings refuted | adversarial verification | **weak signal**, see below |

**Why the refutation rate is weak evidence.** Verifiers were prompted with
"Default to refuted=true when uncertain." That instruction exists to stop the audit
manufacturing findings, and it works, but it makes a high refute rate partly an
artefact of the instrument. Ten of roughly 96 high-severity findings were checked
before the budget ran out.

**Nothing ran a real task.** Every scenario is a model predicting another model.

## 4. The structural finding, deliberately not fixed

> The prompt's only arbitration chain contains no term for delivery. Every
> completion-leaning rule lives in `# method` or `# conduct`, and the chain demotes
> both of those sections by name.

So restraint wins collisions by construction, regardless of the balanced count.
Changing the precedence chain is a much larger edit than two clarifying clauses and
should not ride along with an unmeasured fix.

Suggested shape, recorded for whoever takes it up: the chain names six things that
do *not* authorize action and nothing that does. A symmetric positive clause, along
the lines of an explicitly named target plus an explicitly requested action being
permission to act, would give `act when ready` a domain again.

## 5. What the audit cost, and the bug in it

Designed for roughly 60 agents. **Spawned 326.** The verification fan-out scaled
with model-produced findings and was uncapped: 5 rules by about 26 findings by 3
verifiers. Two runs, roughly 2.7M subagent tokens, session limit hit twice.

Fixed in the harness: verification now caps at the top 4 findings per rule and
logs how many it dropped. A cap that does not announce itself reads as full
coverage.

The original is preserved and annotated at five fault points as a teaching
artefact. Both live outside the repository, in gitignored tooling.

**The rule that came out of it:** any fan-out whose width is decided by a model,
rather than by the script, is unbounded until you bound it.
