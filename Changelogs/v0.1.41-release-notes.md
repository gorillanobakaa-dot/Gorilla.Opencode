# Gorilla OpenCode v0.1.41 — Five bugs from real use — and two of them were not what they looked like

**Date:** 2026-07-27
**Previous version:** v0.1.40

---
## Why This Release Exists
Everything here came from one person actually using v0.1.40 and writing down what went wrong. Five complaints. Two of them turned out to be the program telling the truth badly rather than doing the wrong thing, which is arguably worse: a switch that works but looks broken teaches you to distrust every other switch. The other three were real, and one of them — NVIDIA models being impossible to select — was two separate faults stacked on top of each other, either of which alone would have produced the same dead end.

## What You'll Actually Notice

**Session title in the sidebar**
- BEFORE: The title was whatever the small helper model said, word for word — including its thinking out loud. A real one read: "Here's a possible title, keeping the constraints in mind: **Short and Concise:** **Title:** Your Business Brief", wrapped across three lines.
- AFTER: The reply is treated as untrusted text and reduced to the part that is actually a title. If nothing usable survives, your own first message is used instead.
- Affects: everyone, but most visibly anyone using a small or local model for titles

**Language servers you switched off**
- BEFORE: The sidebar kept listing every server after you disabled them, so it looked like the switch did nothing.
- AFTER: The panel lists only what is actually running, plus a line saying how many are off and where to change it.
- Affects: everyone who has tried to quieten the program down

**Switching a feature off in /context**
- BEFORE: The first press did nothing at all for language servers. The second press worked. This was a real bug, not a display problem.
- AFTER: One press, one change.
- Affects: everyone

**Turning language servers off in bulk**
- BEFORE: Nine configured servers meant nine separate toggles to get a quiet session.
- AFTER: Press L in /context for all of them at once. It switches them back on the same way, and touches nothing else.
- Affects: anyone working on a large codebase

**NVIDIA, Ollama and other local or self-hosted models**
- BEFORE: You could add a key, the connection looked healthy, and not one of its models appeared in the model picker. Re-entering the key and restarting changed nothing.
- AFTER: They appear, listed first, each labelled with the connection it comes from.
- Affects: anyone using NVIDIA NIM, Ollama, LM Studio or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint

**The sign-in link from /login**
- BEFORE: The URL stayed on screen permanently, printed across the interface, overlapping the editor and the status bar for the rest of the session.
- AFTER: It appears in a proper box you can dismiss with esc. Dismissing it does not cancel the sign-in.
- Affects: anyone signing in with Google

**Finding your way around**
- BEFORE: No command list. An unknown command recited a dozen names at you regardless of what you typed.
- AFTER: /help (or /commands) lists everything, grouped by what you are trying to do, each with a plain-language explanation of what it costs or changes. A typo now suggests the command you meant.
- Affects: everyone

**Which folder the program starts in**
- BEFORE: Clicking the desktop icon always started in your home folder. On a machine holding large source trees that put over a million files in the AI's reach before you typed anything, and a /cd only lasted one session.
- AFTER: It asks which folder you want, offering the one you used last. You can tell it to stop asking.
- Affects: everyone who starts the program from the icon rather than a terminal

## Decisions & Why

**The session title falls back to your own first message when the model returns nothing usable.** — A truncated version of what you actually said is more useful than the model's commentary about your request. (benefits: anyone using a small or cheap model for titles; tradeoff: A title may be a clipped version of your sentence rather than a crafted summary.)

**The sidebar says "N off" rather than silently hiding disabled servers.** — Hiding them completely would make a fully quietened setup look like it was never configured. (benefits: everyone; tradeoff: One extra line in the panel.)

**The bulk language-server switch deliberately touches nothing else.** — Working with no language servers is normal. Working with no tools is not, and losing your tool settings to a stray keypress would be a nasty surprise. (benefits: everyone; tradeoff: Turning everything off still needs two separate actions.)

**Where several connections point at the same address, the one carrying a key wins, and the first of two keyed ones is kept.** — Otherwise re-adding a connection can silently steal a working one's models and route them through a broken key. (benefits: anyone who has added the same endpoint more than once while trying to get it working; tradeoff: Duplicate entries are ignored rather than deleted, so tidying them up is still your call.)

**Local models sort to the top of the picker.** — They were sorted below providers you have never touched, because an unranked provider went last. A connection you configured by hand is the one you are looking for. (benefits: anyone running local or self-hosted models; tradeoff: None.)

**The sign-in URL is shown on one long line that scrolls, rather than wrapped to fit the box.** — A URL folded across lines cannot be copied and pasted, which defeats the entire point of displaying it. (benefits: anyone whose browser did not open automatically; tradeoff: The line runs past the edge of the box on a narrow terminal.)

**The command reference is grouped by what you are trying to do, not alphabetically.** — Someone who does not know the command's name cannot look it up alphabetically, and that is exactly the person reading it. (benefits: new users, and anyone who has lost track of the newer features; tradeoff: If you already know the name, you scan a group heading first.)

**The startup folder question can be switched off permanently.** — The standing rule for this project is that anything added has to be possible to turn off. (benefits: people who always work in the same place; tradeoff: None.)

## Deliberately Not Done

- **Deleting the duplicate or broken connection entries found in the reporter's configuration.** — That is user data. The program now ignores the broken duplicates and logs what it ignored, but removing them is the owner's decision.
- **Verifying an API key at the moment you save it.** — The obvious check — asking the endpoint for its model list — proves nothing, because NVIDIA answers that without any authentication at all. The only honest test costs a real request against your quota. Left for a later release rather than guessed at.
- **Restarting language servers when you toggle them.** — Turning them off takes effect at the next launch. Re-rooting a running language server is a bigger change than this release should carry.
- **Tightening the title prompt instead of cleaning up the reply.** — The prompt already says "no meta-text like 'Title:'" and "entire output becomes title". The model ignored both. Asking more politely was not going to work.

## Privacy & Security
No change to what the program collects or sends — it still talks only to the AI providers you configure. Two security-relevant notes. First, connections that share an address are now collapsed, so a key you thought was retired can no longer quietly become the one every request is signed with. Second, a warning from the diagnosis rather than the code: NVIDIA's model-listing endpoint answers without any authentication, so a connection appearing to work is not evidence that your key is valid or even present. Your keys continue to live in ~/.config/gorilla-opencode/env and config.json with owner-only permissions.

## Risks, Backup & Recovery

- **Backup needed:** Your settings are untouched by this upgrade; nothing needs backing up. If you want a fallback binary, keep a copy of the previous one before installing.
- **Install time:** Under a minute.
- **Restart required:** Yes — quit and reopen. Language-server changes in particular only take effect on a fresh start.
- **Rollback:** Install the previous .deb, or restore the binary you copied aside. Your settings files are compatible in both directions; the only new one is an optional flag recording that you asked not to be prompted for a folder at startup, which older versions simply ignore.

## What's Still Broken

- **Keystroke handling in the new dialogs and the startup folder chooser was not tested end-to-end.** (The command reference, the sign-in box and the startup folder chooser.) — workaround: None needed — report anything that misbehaves. The layout and the decision logic are covered by automated checks; only real key presses could not be simulated. — status: A limitation of the development environment, not a known defect: a minimal test program cannot receive simulated keyboard input there either.
- **Language servers switched off in /context only stop at the next launch.** (Anyone toggling them mid-session.) — workaround: Restart the program. — status: Known, deferred.
- **A connection's key is not checked when you save it.** (Anyone pasting a key with a typo or a missing prefix.) — workaround: If a model returns an authorisation error, check the key is complete — including any prefix such as nvapi-. — status: Open. See the note above about why the cheap check is worthless.

## Common Questions

**Q: You said my language servers were being disabled all along. How do you know?**
A: By counting processes. With the same configuration and all nine switched off, no language-server process started at all. With them switched on, that became one clangd, two gopls and five Node-based servers. The switch worked; the panel was reporting the configured list rather than the running one.

**Q: So why could I never select my NVIDIA models?**
A: Two faults at once. The picker built its list of providers from your saved accounts and your environment variables, and a local endpoint is in neither — so all 104 discovered local models were registered and unreachable. Separately, all your NVIDIA entries pointed at the same address and each overwrote the previous one's routing, so the last one won: the entry whose key was missing its nvapi- prefix. Every request through it would have been rejected. Fixing either fault alone would not have been enough.

**Q: Why did adding the key repeatedly not help?**
A: Because each attempt added another entry rather than correcting one, and the newest entry captured the routing. NVIDIA also lists its models without requiring a key, so every attempt looked successful.

**Q: Why did the sign-in URL never go away?**
A: It was printed directly to the terminal while the interface was drawing to the same screen. The text landed on top of the display with no record of it existing, so no redraw could remove it. It is now drawn as part of the interface, which is what makes it dismissible.

**Q: Do I have to answer the folder question every time?**
A: No. Press ctrl+r on the chooser to have it remember, or turn it off in /settings. If you start from a terminal with a folder given on the command line, it never asks.

**Q: Will this upgrade change any of my settings?**
A: No. It reads the same files. One optional flag can be added if you ask it to stop prompting for a folder, and older versions ignore it.

## Bottom Line
This release is almost entirely repair work, prompted by one person using the previous version properly and writing down five things that went wrong. The most useful outcome is that two of the five were not the bugs they appeared to be: language servers really were being switched off, and the panel was simply reporting the wrong list. That distinction matters, because a control that works but looks broken quietly undermines confidence in every other control. The genuinely serious fault was that models from NVIDIA, Ollama and any similar endpoint could not be selected at all — two independent faults stacked, either of which alone produced the same dead end, and no amount of re-entering a key could have cleared it. Alongside the fixes, /help now explains every command in plain language, because the program had reached the point where even the person who asked for the features could no longer keep track of them. Two things are worth being straight about: real key presses in the new dialogs could not be tested in the development environment, and a connection's key is still not verified when you save it, because the cheap way of checking it turns out to prove nothing.

## Subsystem Changes

**NETWORK:** No protocol change. Local-endpoint routing is now one registration per distinct baseURL instead of one per config entry, so duplicate entries no longer issue duplicate /v1/models requests at startup. Diagnostic finding worth recording: NVIDIA's integrate.api.nvidia.com/v1/models answers unauthenticated, so a successful listing is not evidence of a valid credential.

**STORAGE:** config.json gains optional skipWorkspacePrompt (bool, omitempty). Stored inverted deliberately: omitempty drops a false, so an 'ask' bool could never persist as off. loadout.json semantics unchanged; absent keys still mean enabled, which is now honoured consistently by both readers and writers.

**TELEMETRY:** No changes. No collection added or removed.

**OTHER:** TUI stdout discipline established as a rule: while Bubble Tea owns the screen, nothing may write to stdout/stderr outside the renderer. Swept the tree; the surviving fmt.Print calls are the -p non-interactive output path in app.go and the deliberate mouse-mode escape sequences in tui.go.

## Security Posture
No CVEs addressed; no dependency changes; no permission model changes. Attack surface unchanged — the process still talks only to configured providers. Two relevant hardening notes. (1) Collapsing endpoints by baseURL removes a state where a stale or malformed credential silently becomes the one signing every request to that host, which previously happened without any error surfaced to the user. (2) Recorded for operators: an unauthenticated /v1/models response must not be treated as credential validation; NVIDIA's returns 102 models with no Authorization header. Secret files remain 0600 via writeSecretFile; verified the real config.json is still 0600 after this work. One process note: during diagnosis a live NVIDIA API key was read from config.json and echoed into a development transcript; the key should be treated as disclosed and rotated. Diagnostic output touching credential fields must be masked.
