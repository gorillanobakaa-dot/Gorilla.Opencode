# Gorilla OpenCode v0.1.74 — a price tag on big pages, a quota you can scroll back to, and a model that stops inventing its own methods

**Plain-language version below every technical section. Read whichever suits you.**

---

## Why This Release Exists

Three fixes, all from watching the thing fail in front of a user.

A single web page took a session to **88% of its context in one call** — 84,772
tokens — and nobody was told what that cost. The `/usage` quota reading vanished
the moment the next message arrived, so checking it again meant spending another
request against the quota being measured. And a model asked to explain its own
procedure **invented the procedure**: a date filter its tool does not have, JSON
output it does not produce, and link verification it never performed.

**Plain-language version:** One web page quietly ate most of the assistant's
memory and nobody mentioned it. The quota display disappeared as soon as you
looked away, and looking again cost you quota. And when we asked the assistant
how it had done something, it made up a convincing answer instead of checking.

---

## 1 — A price tag on large pages

When a page is fetched it joins the conversation, and **the model re-reads the
whole conversation on every reply.** A large page is therefore not a one-off
charge; it is a subscription for the rest of that session.

The cap that existed protected memory (400 KB) — which is about **100,000
tokens**. It was expressed in the wrong unit for the resource that actually runs
out. That is the same defect already recorded in this repo's trap list, where a
grep tool capped matches at 100 and returned 2.4 MB.

The obvious fix was a tighter cap, and it was wrong. Measured: a converted arXiv
abstract page is ~10,700 tokens and an entire novel is ~42,400. Any cap tight
enough to guarantee cheapness cuts a research paper in half, and half a paper is
worse than none — the model summarises what it received and never mentions the
rest.

So the budget warns early and truncates late:

| size | behaviour |
|---|---|
| under 15,000 tokens | silent — ordinary work is untouched |
| 15,000 – 40,000 | a notice: the token count, that it will be re-sent on every later turn, and the two ways out |
| over 40,000 | cut, loudly, with "the rest was NOT read — do not summarise this as though it were complete" |

The two ways out are always offered: `summarise: true` (condensed locally by
TextRank, costs nothing, declares its compression) or a narrower source from the
fetch ladder.

The original fault was never that 85,000 tokens arrived. It was that **nobody
was told what it cost or offered an alternative.** Making the price visible at
the moment of the decision beats picking a number that is wrong for somebody.

**Plain-language version:** When the assistant reads a web page, that page joins
the conversation — and the AI re-reads the whole conversation every time it
replies. So a big page isn't charged once, it's charged again on every message
after it. Now a note appears saying so, and offers to shorten the document on
your own computer for free. Only genuinely enormous pages are cut short, and it
says clearly when that happens. For scale: the entire text of *Romeo and Juliet*
fits under the limit, so papers and manuals are never touched.

## 2 — `/usage` writes into the scrollback

The quota reading rendered only as a footer toast. It disappeared, and seeing it
again meant typing `/usage` again — **which spends a request against the quota
you are trying to conserve.**

It is now also printed into the terminal history, timestamped:

```
  16:42:07  quota · Gemini 16% · refreshes in 71h 9m
```

The toast stays. The two answer different questions: the footer answers "what is
it now", the history answers "what was it twenty minutes ago", which the footer
cannot do at any price. The timestamp is not decoration — a quota figure without
a time is not a measurement. Two dated readings give you a burn rate; two
undated ones give you nothing.

Printed with `tea.Println`, the only mechanism that writes above the inline
frame. Writing to stdout directly is painted over by the next render with no
record in the renderer, and no redraw can ever clear it — the first entry in
this repo's trap list.

**Plain-language version:** The quota display used to vanish as soon as you
carried on working, and checking it again used up more quota. Now it also stays
in the scroll-back history with the time next to it, so you can scroll up and
see what was left earlier — and work out how fast you are burning through it —
without spending anything.

## 3 — The model no longer invents its own methodology

Observed with GPT-OSS 20B. Asked *"walk me through your procedure"*, it
described:

- a date filter `web_search` does not have
- "the JSON format supplied by the tool" — it returns plain text
- "the top 25 hits" — it had requested 10
- "I used web_fetch to confirm each DOI link" — it had verified none

Asked why it had missed a paper, it blamed an OpenAlex indexing lag. That paper
is the **number one hit for its own title** and has been indexed for eleven
months. The real reason was that it searched "deception" and never tried "lie".

Every structural claim was false, and the account was **more convincing than the
answer it explained** — because it was organised, specific and self-critical. It
reads exactly like accountability.

The `# honesty` section required every progress claim to point at a tool result.
It did not cover claims about *how* the work was done. It does now:

> describing your own process is a progress claim: read the trace, do not
> narrate a plausible procedure: never name a tool, argument or step you did not
> use, and if you do not know why something failed say that instead of inventing
> a cause

+240 bytes, ~60 tokens per coder turn.

**Plain-language version:** We asked the assistant to explain how it had searched
for something. It described settings that don't exist, checks it never did, and
then blamed a technical fault that wasn't real — when the true answer was simply
"I only tried one search word". The explanation sounded more trustworthy than the
original answer, because it was well organised and admitted mistakes. The
assistant is now told that explaining its own work counts as a claim like any
other: look at what actually happened, and if you don't know why something went
wrong, say that.

## Decisions & Why

**A budget, not a cap.** Picking a number that is right for everyone is
impossible; showing the price is not.

**The toast stays.** Replacing it would trade one question for another. Both are
cheap.

**The honesty rule was extended, not rewritten.** The failure was the existing
rule aimed at the post-mortem rather than the work, so the fix is one more line
in the same section.

- **The token budget's thresholds are estimates.** Tokens are counted as
  bytes ÷ 4, good to roughly ±15%. The decision it informs is coarse enough that
  the error does not matter.

## Bottom Line

Big pages now carry a price tag instead of a surprise. The quota reading stays
where you can scroll back to it, with a time attached. And when the assistant
explains how it worked, it is told to read the record rather than write a good
story.
