# Gorilla Opencode v0.1.115: the meter, and the list that had stopped being true

Two things in this release were wrong in the same way, and neither of them
looked wrong. A usage meter showed a number belonging to a different account.
A model list showed models that were not the ones on offer. Both failed
silently, both looked healthy, and both were caught by the owner noticing that
another program on the same machine disagreed.

---

## Plain-language track

### The meter was reading the wrong barrel

If you signed in with a ChatGPT account and typed `/usage`, the program showed
you a weekly allowance and a Google email address. Neither belonged to the
account you were using. They were the numbers for Antigravity, a completely
different free tier, printed under a ChatGPT session because nothing checked
which one you were actually on.

That is worse than showing nothing. A meter that reads full when your tank is
nearly empty is not a broken meter, it is a misleading one, and you would only
find out when the program stopped answering.

The first fix was to stop printing it: only show the meter for the provider you
are actually using. That removed the lie and left a blank.

**A blank was not good enough, and the owner said so.** He had Codex open on the
same Google account, and it showed him 20% of a monthly limit remaining. So the
number existed. We just were not reading it.

### Where the number was hiding

There is no page to ask. OpenAI does not publish a "how much have I used"
endpoint for this sign-in. The numbers ride along on the replies to the requests
you were already making, tucked into the response headers.

That is the good outcome for anyone on a metered connection: reading your usage
costs **nothing at all**, because it arrives on traffic you already paid for.
There is no extra request, no polling, no background check.

The catch is that there is nothing to show until you have asked the model
something. On a fresh session the meter is blank until your first reply comes
back. That is a real limitation and it is not a bug.

### A rounding error that would have panicked you

Once the number was being read, a test caught something small and nasty.

The program stores how much you have **used** and shows how much you have
**left**. Turning one into the other is a subtraction, and the obvious way to
write it in a computer is very slightly wrong. Asking a computer for
`1 - 80/100` does not give you `0.2`. It gives you
`0.19999999999999996`.

That is a difference of one part in fifty thousand billion, and it does not
matter anywhere except at a boundary. The colour scale changes at exactly 20%,
so a user with exactly a fifth of their allowance left would have been shown
**"Banana emergency! Scraping the peel"** instead of the calm amber they had
earned. Every warning level, 20%, 15%, 10% and 5%, sat one step too alarming.

Written the other way round, `(100 - 80) / 100`, it lands on exactly `0.2`. The
test now pins all four boundaries to exact values so it cannot drift back.

### The other thing that had quietly stopped being true

The list of ChatGPT models was typed out by hand, six days ago, from a reading
of what OpenAI was serving that day.

By this week it was wrong. OpenAI was offering four models. We were showing two,
and one of those two, **GPT-5.4-Mini**, is switched off by OpenAI on **31 August
2026**, eight days after this release. That list was about to have a single
working entry on it.

Again the owner caught it from the outside: Codex was running a model called
**GPT-5.6-Luna** on his account, while this program was telling him it was not
available.

### Why the good models were missing, and why that reason was wrong

This is the part worth reading, because it is a mistake about how to know
things rather than a mistake in the code.

The two GPT-5.6 models were not missing by accident. They were **deliberately
excluded**, and there was a confident comment in the source explaining why. It
said those models are flagged `code_mode_only`, that this means they want their
tools presented as a code sandbox rather than as a list, and that offering them
would put two entries in the menu that "sign in fine and then fail on the first
tool call".

Every part of that was reasoned from the **name of the flag**. Nobody had sent a
single request to check. It was a guess, written in the voice of a finding, and
it cost six days of a better model being unavailable.

Reading OpenAI's own published source settled what the flag actually means:
it tells **their** program to hand the model a small toolkit and let it write
code, instead of handing it a long list of tools. It is a choice their client
makes. It is not a rule the server enforces, and nothing stops anyone else
doing it the ordinary way.

Then we stopped reasoning and asked the server. All three models were sent one
ordinary request with one ordinary tool. All three answered correctly.

The old paragraph has been left in the source with the correction printed
underneath it, rather than quietly deleted, so anyone reading later can see what
changed and why.

### So the list is now asked for, not typed

Every time you sign in, and any time you run `/update`, the program asks OpenAI
what it is serving right now and shows you that.

- The **order** is OpenAI's own ranking, not ours.
- Models OpenAI marks as hidden stay hidden. One of the five it returns is an
  internal review tool, not something to chat with.
- When 5.4-Mini switches off next week, it will disappear on its own. Nobody has
  to remember.
- It costs no extra request. The program was already asking that same question
  to check your sign-in worked.

And the sign-in no longer picks your models by name. It used to hard-code "put
the user on GPT-5.5, and put the background jobs on 5.4-Mini", a decision made
once, at sign-in, that then governed your whole session, and which pinned your
background jobs to the model that dies next week. It now asks the list which is
best and which is cheapest.

The cheap one still writes your conversation titles, deliberately. On a free
plan the thing you run out of is **the cooldown, not money**, so the good model
should not be spent naming chats.

### Three smaller things

**Your files stop being sent twice.** When the agent reads a file, then reads
the same file again later, the first copy used to be re-sent with every
following message for the rest of the session. The earlier copy is now replaced
with a one-line note saying a newer identical read appears below. Nothing is
lost: the full text stays in the session record on disk. This only applies to
read-only operations, only when the arguments are identical, and only to results
over 400 characters, because collapsing something small saves nothing.

**"I could not find it" is now a finished job.** The instructions given to the
model listed several ways a task could stop, and every one of them was about
failing to *do* something. None covered failing to *know* something. That gap
matters most where it is most expensive, on a kernel or browser build, where an
invented cause reads exactly like a diagnosed one. One line now says that an
unestablished finding is finished work, not a failure to be pushed through.

**The literature is cited.** The research this work is based on, on context
degradation and on models stopping early, is now listed with sources rather than
paraphrased.

## Developer track

### 1. Quota panel: the wrong provider's numbers

`/usage` rendered the Antigravity weekly quota and the Google account email
regardless of which provider the coder agent was on.

Fixed in two stages, in commit order:

- `fab0503` gates `quotaTierCheckCmd` and `antigravityUsageCmd` on the active
  provider, via `antigravityIsActive()` reading
  `SupportedModels[cfg.Agents[AgentCoder].Model].Provider`. The account line in
  `renderQuotaPanel` became conditional on a non-empty group set, so a gated
  panel does not print a bare `Account:` header with nothing under it.
- `1258fe4` adds the ChatGPT meter, so the gate removes a wrong reading rather
  than leaving a blank.

### 2. `internal/auth/chatgpt_quota.go`

There is no usage endpoint on this backend. Established from the Codex reference
client at `~/Downloads/codex-rust-v0.147.0`
(`codex-rs/codex-api/src/rate_limits.rs`): the figures arrive as `x-codex-*`
response headers. Recorded at the single choke point in
`internal/llm/provider/chatgpt.go`, immediately after `http.DefaultClient.Do`,
so it costs zero extra requests. Best-effort and silent: failing to note a usage
number must never break the request carrying it.

Persisted to `~/.config/gorilla-opencode/chatgpt-quota.json` because the reading
is traffic-driven, and "not known yet" has to stay distinguishable from "no
meter exists".

**Two traps, both encoded:**

The wire field is `used_percent`. This panel reports **remaining**, deliberately
(see the override at `internal/tui/quota_panel.go:16`, written so nobody
confuses 96% left with 96% spent). The conversion lives in exactly one place,
`ChatGPTWindow.Remaining()`.

The window name comes from the wire, never a constant. Codex renders
"Monthly limit" on a free plan, not the weekly pair a paid plan shows.

**The float bug.** The first version of `Remaining()` was `1 - used/100`. In
IEEE-754 binary64, `1 - 80.0/100.0` is `0.19999999999999996`, which is below the
banana ladder's `0.20` boundary. Every alert boundary (80, 85, 90, 95% used) sat
one tier too alarming. `(100 - used) / 100` is exact at every round percentage.
Caught by `TestChatGPTMeterRendersWithBananaLadder`; `TestRemainingInvertsUsedPercent`
now pins all four boundaries with `!=` rather than an epsilon comparison, on
purpose.

**`Label()` was invented, then replaced.** The hand-written first version used
threshold ranges, invented an "Hourly" bucket that does not exist, and had no
5h bucket, which is the most common window a ChatGPT plan reports. It would have
called a 5-hour limit "Hourly" and a 200-minute window "Daily". Ported from
`codex-rs/tui/src/chatwidget/rate_limits.rs`: five named buckets matched within
a 5% tolerance, and a fallback word when nothing matches, never a nearest guess.

### 3. `internal/llm/models/chatgpt_catalogue.go`: the list is fetched

`catalogue_fetch.go` wrote the doctrine on 2026-08-21 ("presence now comes from
the provider") and carved out three OAuth providers as "small, tied to an OAuth
flow, and updated with the binary". This is that carve-out failing exactly as
that file predicted a hand-typed list always fails.

Live `GET /models?client_version=0.147.0`, free plan, 2026-08-23, HTTP 200,
214 KB, 5 entries:

| slug | priority | visibility | tool_mode | ctx | max ctx |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gpt-5.6-terra` | 2 | list | `code_mode_only` | 272000 | 872000 |
| `gpt-5.6-luna` | 3 | list | `code_mode_only` | 272000 | 872000 |
| `gpt-5.5` | 7 | list | null | 272000 | 272000 |
| `gpt-5.4-mini` | 23 | list | null | 272000 | 272000 |
| `codex-auto-review` | 43 | **hide** | `code_mode_only` | 272000 | 872000 |

Ordering comes from `priority` (lower is more prominent), mapped to this
program's `Rank` by **position**, not arithmetic: the numbers are sparse and any
formula over them produces ties or negatives the moment OpenAI renumbers. The
only filter is the backend's `visibility`. `CanReason` comes from
`supported_reasoning_levels` rather than a guess.

`ContextWindow` uses `context_window`, not `max_context_window`. The 872000
figure is a ceiling, not what an ordinary request gets, and a window set too
high produces a rejection indistinguishable from a broken sign-in.

Refresh **replaces** rather than merges, unlike `RefreshAntigravity`. A merge
keeps 5.4-mini in the picker past its 31 Aug retirement, looking selectable and
returning a 400 to whoever picks it. Startup reads the cache from disk only,
never the network; a missing or corrupt cache leaves the built-in list working.

The file does not import `internal/auth`. The caller passes the raw body in,
the same bargain `antigravity_refresh.go` makes.

### 4. The `code_mode_only` correction

`chatgpt.go` excluded the 5.6 pair, predicting they would "sign in fine and then
fail on the first tool call". That was an inference from the flag's name, with
no record of a request having been sent.

From `codex-rs/features/src/lib.rs`, `CodeModeOnly` is documented as
*"Restrict model-visible tools to code mode entrypoints (`exec`, `wait`)"*. A
client-side decision about the contents of the `tools` array. Not a wire format.
`requested_tool_mode()` reads it from model metadata and `effective_tool_mode()`
only ever downgrades `CodeMode`, never `CodeModeOnly`, which is why Codex always
drives these models that way. Nothing obliges any other client to.

One ordinary Responses request per model, one ordinary function schema, free
plan, 2026-08-23:

```
gpt-5.5        HTTP 200  get_weather({"city":"Bucharest"})
gpt-5.6-luna   HTTP 200  get_weather({"city":"Bucharest"})
gpt-5.6-terra  HTTP 200  get_weather({"city":"Bucharest"})
```

Then confirmed in the shipped binary: Terra chained `view` and `find` off a
single prompt (screenshot in the gallery). The original paragraph is retained in
`chatgpt.go` with the correction beneath it.

`PreferredChatGPTModels()` replaces the two constants at the sign-in site
(`applyAgentModels(ChatGPT55, ChatGPT54Mini)`), resolving best and cheapest from
what is registered.

### 5. `internal/llm/provider/supersede.go`

`supersedeStaleReads()` runs inside `cleanMessages`. It keys each read-only
tool call by `name + "\x00" + raw arguments`, and where the same key appears
more than once, replaces all but the last result with a stub naming the tool and
pointing at the session store.

Scope is deliberately narrow: `view` and `find` only, identical arguments only,
results over `supersedeMinContent` (400 bytes) only. The stub is ~160 characters,
so collapsing anything near that size is churn. Nothing mutating is touched, and
nothing is deleted from disk.

### 6. Prompt: `internal/llm/prompt/coder-modern.txt`

6941 → 7164 bytes, +223, +3.2%, roughly +56 tokens on every coder turn. Two
lines changed, none added.

Every permissive rule in the prompt framed stopping as failure handled
gracefully, never as legitimate completion, while the `finish task` conduct rule sat
four lines below as an absolute. A model resolving that seam under load reads
the absolute. And every stop condition covered **doing**: unachievable task, two
attempts, failed build. None covered **knowing**.

```
- a null result is an answer: unachievable and unestablished are finished
  tasks, not failed ones: state the blocker, or say what the evidence does
  not support and what you checked, then stop
```

### Not verified

- **The size of the context saving.** `supersede_test.go` proves the collapse
  happens and that the stub says so. Nobody has measured how many tokens a real
  session saves. The claim in this document is a mechanism, not a number.
- **The 872K context ceiling.** Left alone. A too-high window fails in a way
  indistinguishable from a broken sign-in, which is the wrong failure to invite
  in a release.
- **Any paid ChatGPT plan.** Everything here was checked on a free plan. The
  window labels for weekly and 5h buckets are ported from Codex and unit-tested,
  but no paid account has run them.
- **The retirement date.** Taken from OpenAI's announcement as recorded on
  2026-08-17. The mechanism no longer depends on it: the model disappears when
  the backend stops listing it, whatever the date turns out to be.
